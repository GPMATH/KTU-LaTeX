\section{Research problem, objectives and boundaries}
\subsection{Industry status}
The main use of this technology is to protect PCB core components, such as ICs, capacitors, and resistors, from natural elements such as moisture and other potential contaminants that can pose a reliability risk. This process can be inspected with vision systems; however, conformal coating is transparent, allowing ordinary light to pass through and making inspection complicated. To perform this test, ultraviolet fluorescent light can reveal the coating solution. It is important to note that fluorescence itself cannot estimate coating thickness or other parameters such as curing state, long-term protection, or adhesion. These matters are addressed in IPC-HDBK-830A for processing, coverage, and inspection, and in IPC-A-610J for assembled-board acceptability criteria, which include thickness and whole-board coverage specifications \citep{ipchdbk830a,ipca610j,ipccc830c}.

In this specific scenario, a board of about 70~$\times$~70~mm, populated with several smaller PCBs, is positioned in a fixed position. The camera should target an area of 80~$\times$~80~mm, allowing a 5~mm margin. Ideally, the camera is mounted in a fixed location, although its position may shift in the future. Commercial systems currently add oblique cameras for specific PCB areas \citep{goepelcci,mirteccci}.

\subsection{Research objectives}
The main goal of this work is to assess the possibility of developing a low-cost UV fluorescence station with fixed geometry and image analysis to inspect conformal coating on a 70~$\times$~70~mm PCB panel. The expected KPIs are camera response and excitation; designing a board coverage map from CAD; comparing segmentation under identical conditions; correctly identifying defect types; and auditing CPU latency and cost.

To make a scientific claim, a comparator must be included. Several previous studies have conducted UV bubble inspections using classical methods and CNNs \citep{lee2021cnn,lee2021transfer}. Other studies have claimed that various features can be statistically classified using YOLOv8 tiling and UV intensity \citep{choi2024uv}. Detector networks have also been trained for dewetting and capillary defects \citep{patel2026coverage}. Therefore, neither hybrid processing nor CNN use for conformal coating is novel. In this case, novelty arises from isolating properties and setting actual constraints for defect handling, illumination robustness, and uncertainty in human review.
