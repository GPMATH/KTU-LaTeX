\section{Proposed reference-aware inspection architecture}
\subsection{Design rationale and data flow}
A constrained board-specific coating plan defines \emph{where} coating is required or forbidden; the optical model measures \emph{whether} sufficient fluorescence evidence is observed; and an optional learned branch estimates ambiguous coating structure or defect patterns. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} presents the proposed data flow.

\subsection{Radiometric correction and quality gate}
For an illuminated raw image $I$ with dark frame $D$ and stable flat fluorescent reference $F$ imaged under the same conditions:
\begin{equation}
I_c(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{I(\mathbf{x})-D(\mathbf{x})}{\max(F(\mathbf{x})-D(\mathbf{x}),\epsilon)}\,\overline{(F-D)}.
\label{eq:flatfield}
\end{equation}
Here $\epsilon$ prevents division by near-zero values and $\overline{(F-D)}$ controls overall scaling. Correction is accepted only where the reference is stable, correctly registered and not clipped. It \emph{does not} unshadow occluded surfaces or compensate for all changes in the fluorescent tracer's chemistry. Quality gates flag saturation, insufficient brightness, excessive dark-frame residual, missing board and out-of-focus image. A failed acquisition gate must produce \emph{review/reacquire}, not an automatic PASS.

\subsection{Registration and reference coordinates}
Let $\mathbf{p}$ denote an image pixel and $\mathbf{q}$ its location in the coating-plan coordinate frame. A planar projective transform $\mathbf{q}\sim H\mathbf{p}$ can be estimated from fiducials or stable features using RANSAC \citep{fischler1981ransac,zhang2000calibration}.

Approved mask regions form a disjoint partition of the visible board: $R$ = must-coat, $K$ = must-not-coat, $A$ = optional/allowed, and $U$ = not reliably inspectable/occluded. Every pixel belongs to exactly one region after resolving drawing overlaps.

\subsection{Segmentation output and interpretable indicators}
Define $P(\mathbf{x})\in[0,1]$ as estimated probability or standardized evidence of coating, and $M_\tau(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{1}[P(\mathbf{x})\geq\tau]$ as a binary mask chosen on validation data. Required-zone coverage and forbidden-zone contamination can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
C_R=\frac{|M_\tau\cap R|}{|R|},\qquad
C_K=\frac{|M_\tau\cap K|}{|K|}.
\end{equation}
These are \emph{image-derived ratios}, not coating-thickness measurements. Overall coverage may conceal a critical local hole; therefore identify connected missing regions $H_i\subset R\setminus M_\tau$, measure each area $A_i = n_i s_xs_y$, maximum inscribed radius, contour length and proximity to critical features. \begin{equation}
D=\begin{cases}
\mathrm{REVIEW}, & Q=0 \text{ or uncertainty $>u_{\max}$},\\
\mathrm{FAIL}, & \exists\text{ validated actionable region violating $R$ or $K$ rules},\\
\mathrm{PASS}, & \text{otherwise within defined visible inspection scope},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ summarizes acquisition, registration and model validity. A PASS must be explicitly scoped to inspected visible surfaces and validated defect categories.

\subsection{Model selection and ablation logic}
The segmentation branch should be replaceable: a calibrated HSV/intensity threshold, lightweight U-Net, MobileNetV3 decoder, SegFormer-B0 or a learned anomaly score. The reference-comparison branch must use identical region rules and optical inputs where possible across experiments. Ablations should remove individual modules one at a time: (A) flat-field correction; (B) geometric registration; (C) the learned mask; (D) the reference regions; (E) multi-angle illumination; and (F) uncertainty handling.
