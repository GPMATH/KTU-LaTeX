\section{Conclusions}

This literature analysis shows that the thesis methodology can be supported by a modest, but solid claim: the combination of fNIRS and machine learning is currently more effective for classifying haemodynamic patterns than clinical diagnosis.

To that end, the updated methodology considers the following factors: short-distance channel regression for eliminating extracerebral effects, preservation of channel-by-time representation, distinction of relative haemodynamic patterns, and reporting of oxygenation only when explicitly estimated by the instrument and algorithm.

The future research should address leakage problems, employ nested hyperparameter optimization, perform preprocessing in a leakage-safe manner, use robust baselines, and implement model interpretability techniques.
